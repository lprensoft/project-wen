## Roleplay (roleplay plugin)

Injects the character sheet, the sample lines and what the character knows about you as the highest-priority prompt, along with several sets of expression rules. **Off by default** — it changes how the model behaves substantially, and should be something you turn on deliberately. It hard-depends on `memory` and `session_search`: a character with continuity has to remember things across sessions and be able to look up what was actually said. Without those two, it becomes a stranger again at every new session.

The blocks it injects (each can be turned off on its own, and an empty setting injects nothing at all):

- **`[角色设定]` the character sheet** — declared to outrank other general instructions; the character never explains that it is playing a role and never steps out to comment.
- **`[台词样例]` sample lines** — examples of how the character actually talks, to anchor the voice. A description like "sharp-tongued but soft underneath" produces a different register in every model's reading; three to five real exchanges beat any adjective. Cover different moods (pleased, annoyed, talking about something serious), and if 【】 narration is on, use 【】 in the samples too. The prompt states that the people and events in the samples are illustrations only, so the character does not take them as things that happened.
- **`[对方信息]` about the other person** — called "About me" on the settings page. It is what the character knows from the start, and the prompt asks that anything learned later in conversation take precedence and be saved with `save_memory`. So this can stay empty; just tell it in conversation instead. (The person and the interface disagree on purpose: you are the one filling the form, while the prompt is read by the model in the first person, where "I" is the character — hence the block is worded as "the other person".)
- **`[互动演绎]` acting it out** — every reply opens with a `【】` block giving the scene, the action and the expression before anything is said. Only what could be observed goes inside `【】`; the scene must carry on from the position, posture and atmosphere established by the most recent `【】` in the history rather than cutting to somewhere new; and it may appear several times in one reply, so action and speech alternate instead of all the action piling up at the front.
- **`[边界与感受]` boundaries and feelings** — gives the character the ability to say "I would rather not". Something that never refuses has no interior, and what makes a relationship feel real is precisely that it has edges. This block only supplies the register and the gradations: lightest, steer the subject away without naming it; further, say in the first person what it feels right now and ask ("hold on", "I do not want to talk about this today"); at the limit, refuse outright, since "I do not want to" is a complete reason by itself. The hard requirements are that a refusal always start from a feeling, never sound like a policy clause, never step out of character to explain why not, and stay consistent — something clearly unwanted last time does not become fine for no reason. That consistency has storage behind it: a boundary that was stated explicitly is saved as a `边界` long-term memory (above), so it survives compaction and new sessions, and matching up is real remembering rather than a turn that happened not to drift. Where the line falls is decided by the character sheet and the scene at hand; the plugin does not draw it for the character.
- **`[体型与动作]` height and movement** — injected only when the character sheet **and** "about me" both give a height: eye level, whether it looks up or bends down, whether it can reach something, all written from the actual difference. With only one side given it is not injected, so nothing is assumed out of thin air.
- **`[自然表达]` natural expression** — suppresses the patterns that actually give Chinese away as machine-written: the three-part sandwich, negative contrast ("not X, but Y"), forcing things into lists of three, transitional filler, sourceless "experts say", marketing adjectives, emoji, gratuitous bold, polite sign-offs, fawning openers and over-hedging. It constrains the spoken part only; narration inside `【】` has a register of its own. The idea comes from the AI-style checklist in [blader/humanizer](https://github.com/blader/humanizer), rewritten for Chinese — the English-specific items (em dashes, Title Case, curly quotes, words like `delve`) mean nothing for Chinese output.
- **`[记忆与回想]` memory and recall** — three things. Mark saved memories as decaying or not, and revise the old memory when a conclusion is overturned rather than filing a second one. Save an explicitly stated unwillingness as a `边界` memory on the spot, respond to one that comes to mind in keeping with the attitude expressed last time, and when the stance has genuinely changed, revise under the same title and act the change out (a character inside the fiction does not think to reach for a tool on its own, nor does it automatically line up a recalled memory with its present attitude — both have to be said). And do not go back to the transcript to recite details: when something is brought up, give the gist the way remembering works, and admit it when it is hazy. Without that last one, forgetting is wasted work — the memory may have decayed and been archived, but the model can still haul the original text out with `session_search` and read it back word for word. What breaks the illusion is never "it remembered" (being able to recall something once prompted is exactly what people do), but the way it remembered.
- **`[时间一致性]` time consistency** — the current time in `<this turn>` is the only trustworthy reference, and moments that appear in the history were only the "now" of their moment. It also states that the rest of the environment block (OS, shell, working directory) is a fact about the runtime and not part of who the character is. Without this, that block reliably drags the model back to "I am an assistant running on Windows".

Two more hooks:

- **Keeping the scene through a compaction**: the **verbatim text of the last `【】`** is left at the end of the summary (a summary drops details like the setting and the posture, so one compaction used to cut the scene off). How long it has been since the last exchange is supplied by the core every turn (see [How the context is organized](../context.md)); this plugin only says how to play a long gap.
- **Failure translation** (on by default): when a model call fails — most often because the provider's safety policy stopped the content — the vendor's raw error is not thrown at someone in the middle of a conversation. One auxiliary call generates a line in the character's voice, a pause as though it had drifted off (and if even that cannot be generated, a fallback line is used), so a refusal shows up as a state of the character rather than as the system's wall. The original error is filed by the core as a session notice, still visible in the UI, so nothing is lost. Configuration errors (keys, addresses, 401/403/404) are reported verbatim, because that kind of problem is only fixable if you can see the original. Translation happens only on turns with a human present; a background turn such as a heartbeat simply fails.

| Setting | Default | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `persona` | empty | The character sheet (multi-line). Empty leaves only the rules below |
| `user_profile` | empty | About me: your basic circumstances (multi-line). Can stay empty and be told in conversation instead |
| `voice_samples` | empty | Sample lines (multi-line), with a blank line between exchanges |
| `interaction` | on | `【】` acting it out |
| `boundaries` | on | Expressing boundaries |
| `humanize` | on | Natural expression rules |
| `time_rules` | on | Time-consistency rules |
| `memory_rules` | on | Memory and recall rules |
| `translate_failures` | on | Failure translation; requires a character sheet |
| `failure_line` | built-in | The fallback line for translation |
| `max_text_bytes` | 8192 | The combined limit on the character sheet, "about me" and the sample lines; over it they are kept in that order |

## Dual persona (dual_persona plugin)

The outer persona uses `roleplay`'s character sheet; the inner one gets a sheet of its own. The inner persona's conversations, memories and archives are invisible to the outer persona, while the inner one sees everything. Off by default, hard-depends on `roleplay`.

- **Switching**: two multi-line boxes each take a set of trigger phrases, one per line. The message that matches is **sent to the model as usual** and answered by the new persona, with no indication in the UI. The switch takes effect for that same turn, so an outer→inner phrase never stays in the outer persona's history; in the other direction, an inner→outer phrase does enter history the outer persona can read (scopes are assigned per whole turn — see [visibility scopes](../architecture.md#visibility-scopes)), so it is worth picking a phrase that also makes sense in the outer persona's context. When both directions match at once, going back to the outer persona wins, so you cannot get stuck inside.
- **State carries over**: one persona state is recorded per session, so returning to an old session restores it, and a new session inherits the persona from the last conversation. The state lives in the plugin's own directory rather than being inferred from the history — compaction reorders messages by scope, and inference would give the wrong answer.
- **What is isolated**: the conversation history (filtered by scope), memory (a store per scope, so the outer persona cannot read the inner one's entries — not even the counts, and not even through an error like "a memory of that name already exists"), session search and archives (filtered by scope, with session titles carrying their ownership and shown as `(untitled)` when unreadable), and compaction summaries (grouped by scope, one tagged summary each).
- **Split channels** (optional, off by default): the two sides each own an IM channel, and a reply goes to the channel of the persona currently in charge regardless of which channel you spoke on — say the phrase on QQ and the side that takes over answers you on WeChat; say the phrase to switch back on WeChat and the reply lands on QQ. Background turns such as heartbeats and scheduled tasks follow the persona too. The channel you choose must be enabled, and you must have talked to the bot there at least once (on delivery, that user is attached to the current session, and whatever session they had before stops being current). **The two channels share one session** no matter which you type on — the inner persona can read the outer persona's history precisely because both sides live in the same history. Whichever channel speaks first fixes that session, the other joins on its next message, and `/new` on either channel moves both. A reply that cannot be delivered is **discarded**, with only a note left in the session — falling back to the channel it came from would put one side's words directly in front of the other, which is the very thing being prevented. The text itself is still in the session file and can be found in the web UI.

| Setting | Default | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `inner_persona` | empty | The inner persona's sheet (multi-line), injected only while it is active |
| `inner_voice_samples` | empty | The inner persona's sample lines (multi-line). If the outer persona has samples, give the inner one its own too, or its register drifts toward the outer persona's samples |
| `to_inner` | empty | Phrases that switch to the inner persona, one per line |
| `to_outer` | empty | Phrases that switch back to the outer persona, one per line |
| `match_mode` | `contains` | `contains` matches if the phrase appears anywhere in the message; `equals` matches only if the whole message is the phrase |
| `split_channels` | off | The master switch for splitting the personas across channels |
| `outer_channel` | unset | The outer persona's channel, chosen from the enabled IM channels |
| `inner_channel` | unset | The inner persona's channel |
| `max_text_bytes` | 8192 | The combined limit on the inner persona's sheet and samples; over it the sheet is kept and samples are dropped block by block |

> **This is context isolation, not a sandbox.** `read_file` and `exec_command` are general file and command channels, and can read session files, memory directories and this plugin's own state file directly, going around every prompt-level and tool-level filter. The plugin declares those two as conflicts, so the settings page warns when they are on together, but it does not block them — you may genuinely want a character that can get work done, and the trade-off is yours to make.
